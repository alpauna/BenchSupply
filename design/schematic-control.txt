BENCH SUPPLY - CONTROL BOARD
Pico, the barrier drive, fan staging, sensors, relay sequencing
===============================================================================

  ** THE OPTOCOUPLERS ARE NOT ON THIS BOARD. **

  They live on the converter boards, one set per channel. That is deliberate
  and it is what keeps this board simple: every signal leaving here is
  referenced to the Pico's ground, including the LED drive going out and the
  phototransistor collector coming back. The isolation happens over there.

  Put the optos here instead and this board would carry THREE isolated
  domains - Pico, channel A, channel B - with barrier slots between them.
  There is no reason to.

  So: ONE ground domain on this board. The 5 V module feeds it, and the
  module's own transformer is what isolates the whole thing from mains.


1. GPIO BUDGET - CHECK THIS BEFORE ADDING ANYTHING
--------------------------------------------------

  SPI0 to channel A: SCK MOSI MISO CS_SW CS_AD     5
  SPI1 to channel B: SCK MOSI MISO CS_SW CS_AD     5
  RLY_MAIN drive x2                                2
  NE556 trigger x2 (the RLY_SOFT one-shots)        2
  fan 2 and fan 3 drive                            2
  1-Wire bus (3 sensors)                           1
  display, I2C                                     2
  encoder A: A B push                              3
  encoder B: A B push                              3
  --------------------------------------------------
  TOTAL                                           25   of 26. ONE SPARE.

  Two things buy room if it gets tighter:
    - share one SPI across both channels, picking with CS. Costs 10 mA of
      GPIO drive into two opto LEDs at once instead of 5 - inside the
      RP2040's 12 mA, but with nothing left.
    - one encoder plus a channel-select button instead of two.

  Keeping SPI0 and SPI1 separate is worth the pins: the channels are
  isolated from each other, and a shared clock means one GPIO driving LEDs
  in two different isolated domains.


2. POWER
--------

   from the 5 V module          +5V rail
   (back wall, always on        o---+-------+--------+--------+--------+
    with the rocker)                |       |        |        |        |
        +5V o--------+----------- Pico    fans    relay    opto     1-Wire
                     |            VSYS    (3)     coils    LEDs     pull-up
        GND o--------+                     |       (4)
                     |                     |
                    ===  470u + 100n     ** fans get their own feed or their
                     |                      own decoupling: commutation spikes
                    GND                     land on the rail the ADC reads
                                            millivolts of shunt signal from **

   ** VSYS, PIN 39. NOT VBUS. **
   VBUS is the USB connector's 5 V with nothing in between, so driving it
   back-feeds whatever host is plugged in. USB reaches VSYS through the
   on-board Schottky D1, so a cable can stay connected for programming while
   the module's 5 V holds VSYS up. Ground on pin 38.

   Budget (docs/calc/control.py):
     Pico 100 mA + 3 fans 600 + opto LEDs 50 + 4 relay coils 320 = 1070 mA
     against the module's 3 A. 2.8x.


3. THE BARRIER DRIVE - LOGIC OUT, LOGIC BACK
--------------------------------------------

   Per channel, to a 6-pin header. All Pico-referenced.

     GP -> SCK    ---->|  to OK1 LED anode on the converter board
     GP -> MOSI   ---->|  to OK2
     GP -> CS_SW  ---->|  to OK3
     GP -> CS_AD  ---->|  to OK4
     GP <- MISO   <----|  from OK5 phototransistor collector
     GND ------------->|

   The 330R LED resistors are on the converter board with their optos.
   ** The MISO pull-up (10k to 3V3) belongs HERE **, on the Pico side -
   it is the only one of the five that is not in an isolated domain.

   Clock at a few kHz. A 10k pull-up and a phototransistor make edges tens
   of microseconds long, so 10 bits takes single-digit milliseconds. That is
   invisible to someone turning a knob, and trying to run it at SPI speeds
   will simply not work.


4. FANS
-------

   FAN1 o----+------------------------------o +5V     ALWAYS ON. NO SWITCH.
             |
            D1 flyback, cathode to +5V

   FAN2 o----+----[ AO3400 on a 2N7002 board ]---- GND
             |            gate <- GP, 220R + 10k pulldown
            D2

   FAN3 o----+----[ AO3400 on a 2N7002 board ]---- GND
             |
            D3

   ** FAN 1 IS HARDWIRED. ** A box shedding 108 W with no fans because the
   Pico is halted - or because someone is reflashing it - is a fire and not
   a bug. Baseline cooling has no software in the path.

   AO3400, not the 2N7002 the boards ship with: a 200 mA fan is 1.7x a
   2N7002's 115 mA rating, and 2-3x that at startup. Same SOT-23 pinout
   (G-S-D), same 220R and 10k, a transistor swap rather than a redesign.

   Flyback diode on EVERY fan. The driver board does not carry one, and it
   matters more with an AO3400's 30 V than the 2N7002's 60.


5. RELAY SEQUENCING - TWO LINES PER CHANNEL, NOT THREE
------------------------------------------------------

   GP -> RLY_MAIN_A  --> FET on the mains board -> RLY_MAIN coil
   GP -> TRIG_A      --> NE556 trigger, active low

   Same for B.

   The Pico does NOT drive RLY_SOFT. It triggers a monostable that does, and
   that monostable drops the relay out by itself whether or not anything
   else worked. See schematic-mains.txt section 3.

   Sequence per channel:  TRIG -> (RLY_SOFT closes, inrush limited by 10R)
                          +250 ms -> RLY_MAIN closes, R bypassed
                          +300 ms -> RLY_SOFT drops out on its own

   Stagger the two channels ~200 ms apart. Firing both together makes the
   inrush peaks add - 34 A instead of 17 - and the Pico is triggering them
   anyway, so it costs one line of firmware.

   A 3.3 V GPIO pulled low triggers a 5 V 556 correctly: its threshold is
   1/3 Vcc = 1.67 V. No level shifter needed on the trigger.


6. TEMPERATURE - 1-WIRE, ONE PIN FOR ALL OF IT
----------------------------------------------

   3V3 o---[ 4k7 ]---+---o DQ ---- GP
                     |
                     +---- DS18B20 probe 1 : pass-element heatsink, channel A
                     +---- DS18B20 probe 2 : pass-element heatsink, channel B
                     +---- DS18B20 probe 3 : intake air

   Stainless probes, and the packaging is the point: they are electrically
   isolated BY CONSTRUCTION. The heatsinks are tied to their own channels'
   grounds, so channel A's sink and channel B's sink sit up to 60 V apart
   once the outputs are stacked. A metal-cased sensor bolted to each, both
   wired back here, would BRIDGE THE TWO CHANNELS.

   (Good for a 60 V heatsink. Not safety insulation - not against mains.)

   The heatsink probe drives the staging; it leads everything else by a long
   way. The intake probe goes in the fan stream and away from any heatsink
   or it reads its own self-heating.

     fan 2   on 45 C   off 40 C
     fan 3   on 55 C   off 48 C

   Thresholds with hysteresis, NOT "temperature still rising" - a derivative
   trigger fires on every load step and lid opening and is unloggable to
   tune. And a FAILED READ - CRC error, missing sensor, shorted bus - must
   stage UP. Treat it as hot. Not as cold, and not as hold-the-last-value.


7. USER INTERFACE
-----------------

   Display   I2C, 2 pins, on a header. SSD1306-class or similar.
   Encoders  one per channel: A, B, push. 3 pins each.

   Both are OPEN - see docs/control.md. What is fixed is that they cost 8
   GPIOs between them and there is only one spare.


8. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT ON THIS BOARD
-----------------------------------------

  * No optocouplers. They are on the converter boards - see the top.
  * No mains. Not one net on this board is above 5 V.
  * No relay coils or their FETs. Those are on the mains board, next to
    the contacts they switch.
  * No current sensing. The INAs are on the converter boards, at the shunts,
    and their readings arrive as ADC counts over the barrier.
