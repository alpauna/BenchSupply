BENCH SUPPLY - MAINS ENTRY, DISTRIBUTION AND SOFT START
120 VAC in, two toroid primaries out, plus the 5 V control module
===============================================================================

  Lives on the BACK WALL behind the printed shield. This is the only mains
  in the box. Everything downstream of the transformers is floating.

  The inlet feeds THREE loads, which is why it is a distribution point and
  not just a socket: toroid A, toroid B, and the 5 V control module.


1. ENTRY AND DISTRIBUTION
-------------------------

   IEC C14 inlet
   with rocker + fuse drawer
        L o-----/ /-----+-------------------+-------------------+
                F0      |                   |                   |
             10 A T    F1                  F2                  F3
             250 V    5 A T               5 A T               1 A T
             (in the  250 V               250 V               250 V
              drawer)  |                   |                   |
                       |                   |                   |
                    [soft start A]      [soft start B]         |
                       |                   |                   |
                       |                   |                   |
        N o------------+---+---------------+---+---------------+---+
                           |                   |                   |
                       toroid A            toroid B            5 V module
                       primary             primary             ACL / ACN
                       (brown 18AWG)                           (always on)

        E o--------------------+--> supply earth terminals, star point
                               +--> any exposed metalwork
                                    The enclosure is plastic, but the C14's
                                    earth pin still has to go somewhere.

  All three are 5 x 20 mm CARTRIDGE, 250 V. Mains cannot use blade fuses.
  Values allow for derating - a cartridge runs at ~89% of rating at the 45 C
  inside this box:

      F0  10 A T   carries 5.99 A   both channels at full output at once
      F1  5 A T    carries 2.92 A   4 A derates to 3.6 and blows on a warm day
      F2  5 A T    carries 2.92 A
      F3  1 A T    carries 0.16 A   the 5 V module, 5.6x margin

  F0 is also the inlet module's own 10 A ceiling, so there is no room above
  it. It only binds with both channels at full output simultaneously, and
  each leg has its own fuse - but it is the reason not to grow the load.

  ** The 5 V module is ALWAYS ON with the rocker, ahead of any relay. **
  It has to be: it powers the Pico that closes the relays, and the fan that
  runs whenever the box is powered.


2. SOFT START - TWO STAGE, ONE PER TRANSFORMER
---------------------------------------------

                    RLY_SOFT           R 10R 20W
       from F1 o--+-----/ o------------[\/\/\]------+-----o to primary
                  |                                 |
                  |          RLY_MAIN               |
                  +-------------/ o-----------------+

  Two parallel paths. Soft first, then main.

  Sequence:
    1. rocker on            -> 5 V module up, Pico boots, fan 1 runs
    2. RLY_SOFT closes      -> primary energises THROUGH R, inrush limited
    3. ~250 ms later
       RLY_MAIN closes      -> R bypassed, running normally
    4. RLY_SOFT drops out   -> R out of circuit entirely

  Without R, a 350 VA toroid's inrush is 10-100x its 2.92 A rating:

      10x ->   29 A      30x ->   88 A      100x ->  292 A

  depending on where in the cycle you switch and on the residual flux. That
  is what welds switch contacts and trips breakers. With 10 R:

       R     peak inrush   W while in circuit   J over 300 ms
     4.7R          36 A                 40 W            12 J
    10.0R          17 A                 85 W            26 J
    22.0R           8 A                187 W            56 J


3. RLY_SOFT IS A ONE-SHOT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE SAFETY ARGUMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------

  ** RLY_SOFT IS DRIVEN BY A 555 MONOSTABLE, ~300 ms. NOT BY A LEVEL. **

  It drops out by itself whether or not anything else worked:

    RLY_MAIN closes in time -> R bypassed, running. Normal.
    RLY_MAIN never closes   -> RLY_SOFT drops at 300 ms and the box is
                               simply OFF. Nothing burns. Nothing to detect,
                               nothing to recover from.

  The resistor can only ever be in circuit for one monostable period. Worst
  case is 85 W x 0.3 s = 26 J per attempt, and even retriggered continuously
  that is a warm 20 W wirewound rather than a fire.

  This is why the two-stage arrangement beats a series main with a parallel
  bypass. In that one, RLY_MAIN closing without RLY_BYP following leaves R
  at 85 W indefinitely, and the best you can do is make it unlikely with a
  timer. Here the dangerous state cannot persist by construction.

  Same principle as fan 1 being hardwired and as "switches open = 2 V": the
  failure direction is structural, not a promise.

  RLY_MAIN may be the Pico's. Its failure is benign now - the box just does
  not come on.


3a. CONTACT DUTIES - NEITHER RELAY SWITCHES A COLD TOROID
---------------------------------------------------------

  RLY_SOFT  closes into inrush limited by R: 17 A peak.
            Carries 2.92 A for 300 ms only -> a SMALL relay will do.
  RLY_MAIN  closes into an ALREADY ENERGISED transformer, so it sees no
            inrush at all, then carries 2.92 A continuously.

  That is the second advantage of this topology. With a series main plus
  parallel bypass, RLY_MAIN takes the limited inrush AND the continuous
  current, so it has to be the bigger part.


4. STAGGER THE TWO TRANSFORMERS
-------------------------------

  Firing both RLY_SOFTs together makes the inrush peaks add - up to 34 A.
  Stagger the two sequences ~200 ms apart and it stays at 17 A, with the
  second starting into a mains already loaded. The Pico is triggering them
  anyway, so this costs one line of firmware.


5. COILS
--------

  4 relays, 5 V coils, ~80 mA each:

      Pico                         100 mA
      3 fans                       600 mA
      opto LEDs                     50 mA
      2 x RLY_MAIN                 160 mA   continuous while running
      2 x RLY_SOFT                 160 mA   300 ms only, never both
      TOTAL                       1070 mA   against the module's 3 A = 2.8x

  Contacts: 10 A / 250 VAC is ample for RLY_MAIN. RLY_SOFT can be smaller -
  it carries 2.92 A for 300 ms and never switches a cold toroid.

  Drive: the 2N7002 boards WITH THE AO3400 SWAP. 80 mA is most of a 2N7002's
  115 mA rating before you count the turn-off spike.
  ** Flyback diode across every coil. ** A relay coil is far more inductive
  than a fan, and the board does not carry one.
